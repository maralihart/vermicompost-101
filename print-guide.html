Dry is only for the two buffer trays. Worm habitat must stay damp and aerated.

Setup

  1. Assemble securely on a level surface using the Classic manual supplied with the unit. Confirm every tray seats safely.
  2. Load Tray 1 with about 2 inches of dry peat. It is a backup absorbent layer—not permission to run the bin wet.
  3. Start Tray 2 with damp shredded paper/cardboard and hydrated coir or peat. Add red wigglers and a modest covered feeding.
  4. Add active trays as the working tray fills. Keep material loose; if using reviewer-suggested corner spacers, verify the stack cannot wobble.
  5. Finish with Tray 5 holding about 2 inches of dry peat, then fit the lid. Never feed either buffer tray.
Moisture target: damp like a wrung-out sponge or potting soil—no pooling, steady dripping, sour smell, or compressed sludge.

Weekly: feed the surface, do not dig

1

Prepare

Cut scraps small. Pair wet “greens” with browns such as shredded paper, cardboard, or coir. Hydrate cover material to potting-soil consistency.

2

Place one pile

Put a modest food pile on the surface in one spot or corner. Do not turn the tray or bury scraps through finished material.

3

Cover completely

Add a couple handfuls of damp coir/peat or at least 1 inch of bedding. No food should remain exposed.

4

Check before more

If last week’s food remains, odors appear, or moisture is high, pause or reduce feeding. Keep both dry-peat trays undisturbed.

Moisture & fruit-fly corrections

Too wet / condensation
Pause wet food; add dry shredded bedding to the active tray; loosen gently for air; check drainage and feeding volume.
Too dry
Mist only the active bedding or its paper cover. Do not wet the dry buffer trays.
Fruit flies
Remove exposed/rotting excess, cover every feeding, refresh the top dry-peat barrier, and use an apple-cider-vinegar trap nearby.
Persistent fly outbreak
Stop feeding briefly. University guidance supports a temporary 3 cm dry-peat surface layer over food, left undisturbed about two weeks.

Harvest the lowest active tray

Ready: all three active trays are in use and Tray 2 is dark, mostly uniform, and crumbly. Condition—not a promised calendar—is the trigger.

  1. Remove the lid and top dry-peat tray; set them aside.
  2. Move the ready active tray to the exposed top of the working stack.
  3. In bright ambient light, fluff the material so worms can move down.
  4. Remove castings gradually from the top. Pause as worms retreat into the tray below.
  5. Return the emptied tray to active service; restore the top dry-peat tray and lid.

Urgent troubleshooting

Sour or rotten odorStop feeding. Remove obvious excess, add dry browns, loosen compaction, and restore airflow.

Pooled liquid / repeated dripsCorrect the wet active tray immediately. Empty the base. Treat drainage as leachate, not brewed worm tea.

Worms escapingCheck heat, flooding, dryness, acidity, fresh-food overload, and blocked airflow. Correct conditions before adding food.

Stack leaning or compressedUnload safely, level the base, reseat trays, and remove any spacer that causes wobble. Do not overfill.

Extreme temperatureMove the unit out of heat or hard frost. Anecdotal overwintering is not a guarantee.

Peat cautionKeep large amounts of dry acidic peat out of active bedding. Never add quicklime or hydrated lime.
