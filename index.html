The exact stack

Keep the buffers and habitat distinct

“Dry peat” applies only to the top and lowest removable trays. Worm habitat must stay damp and breathable; the cover used on food is hydrated before it enters an active tray.

Dry buffer material

Two dedicated peat trays

About 2 inches of loose, dry peat in each. The top blocks access to food for fruit flies. The lowest absorbs only occasional drips. Do not feed these trays or intentionally house worms in them.

Damp living habitat

Three working trays

Shredded paper/cardboard and/or coir or peat, damp like a wrung-out sponge and kept loose. Worms, food, microbes, and developing castings belong here.

Hydrated feeding cover

Potting-soil consistency

Hydrate a small bucket of peat or coir before feeding week. Use a couple handfuls to cover the fresh surface pile. This is moist cover—not material taken from either dry buffer tray.

Peat caution: Peat is naturally acidic. Keep dry buffer peat separate from active bedding, watch worm behavior, and do not routinely mix large amounts of dry peat into working trays. Never add quicklime or hydrated lime.

Set up

Build from the base upward

Use the Classic’s supplied parts as its manual directs. Place the assembled system level before adding weight.

  1. 01

    Prepare the hardware

    Set the collection base and legs on a level, protected surface. Install the supplied strainer/separator components according to the Classic manual.

  2. 02

    Add the lower dry tray

    Set the lowest removable tray above the base and add about 2 inches of loose, dry peat. This tray stays worm-free and normally never moves.

  3. 03

    Start working tray 1

    Add damp shredded paper/cardboard and/or hydrated coir, then red wigglers. Keep it moist, airy, and free of standing water.

  4. 04

    Bring trays 2 and 3 online

    Add prepared working trays as capacity is needed. Let worms move between engaged trays; keep all active bedding damp and avoid heavy, compressed loads.

  5. 05

    Add the top dry tray

    Reserve the fifth tray for about 2 inches of dry peat, then fit the lid. It is a physical fly barrier, not a bedding cap inside the active tray.

  6. 06

    Check the finished stack

    Confirm every tray is seated, the tower is vertical, vents are clear, and the base is empty. Recheck stability as trays fill.

Optional reviewer modification

Four-corner anti-compaction spacers

The reviewer placed one 2–3 inch spacer at each corner between loaded working trays so upper-tray weight would not crush lower material and restrict oxygen. PVC was their choice; replaceable wood blocks were another suggestion.

Stability comes first. These are not specified VermiHut parts. Use four equal spacers only if trays remain securely engaged, level, and resistant to wobble. Remove them if they interfere with fit or make the increasingly top-heavy stack unstable. Follow the unit’s load limits.

Primary weekly method

Pile, cover, close. Do not dig.

This is the preferred routine from the detailed review: one localized surface pile, covered with hydrated peat or coir. It avoids turning through half-decomposed food and disturbing worms.

  1. 1

    Hydrate cover ahead

    About a week before feeding, moisten a small bucket of peat or coir to potting-soil consistency: damp and loose, not dripping.

  2. 2

    Collect and prepare scraps

    Save vegetable scraps, tea leaves, and coffee grounds. Cut large pieces smaller. Add browns such as damp shredded paper or coir alongside wet “greens.”

  3. 3

    Make one surface pile

    Once a week, place a modest pile in one spot or corner of the top active working tray. Do not bury it and do not dig through the tray.

  4. 4

    Cover the pile

    Lay a couple handfuls of the pre-hydrated peat/coir over the food so no scraps remain exposed. Replace the dry-peat tray and lid.

Good regular inputs

  • Vegetable trimmings and wilted greens
  • Most fruit scraps, in manageable amounts
  • Coffee grounds and tea leaves; remove plastic bags and staples
  • Damp shredded paper, cardboard, or coir as browns

Leave these out

  • Meat, fish, dairy, oils, and greasy food
  • Pet waste, plastic, produce stickers, and glossy paper
  • Large amounts of citrus, onion, garlic, or spicy food
  • Any feeding in either dry-peat buffer tray

Pace by observation: If the previous pile is still substantial, wait or feed less. Condition and worm activity matter more than a fixed weight or calendar promise.

Care

Damp habitat, dry buffers, empty base

✊

Squeeze active bedding

It should feel cool and clump lightly without running water. Mist only for small corrections. Never let worm habitat dry out.

≋

Keep it airy

Loosen only compacted active material, keep vents clear, add browns with wet scraps, and reduce tray loading if lower trays compress.

◌

Inspect both buffers

Keep the top peat loose and dry. If the lower peat repeatedly gets wet, treat that as a system problem—not normal collection.

⌂

Protect the stack

Keep it level, shaded, protected from freezing and excessive heat, and stable as it becomes top-heavy. Outdoor overwintering is not guaranteed.

!

Drainage is a warning, not a harvest

Liquid in the base is leachate, not brewed worm tea. Do not collect or recommend it for plants. Remove it as waste, then reduce wet food or added water, add absorbent browns to working bedding, improve airflow, and check for compaction. Sour odor, pooling, or repeatedly saturated lower peat means moisture management has failed.

Exact light-driven harvest

Move the lowest mature working tray to the top

Harvest when all three working trays are in use and the lowest one—immediately above the permanent lower peat tray—is dark, mostly uniform, crumbly, and has few recognizable scraps. Do not promise a fixed schedule.

  1. 1

    Remove the cap

    Lift off the lid and top dry-peat tray together. Set them aside without mixing that dry peat into the working system.

  2. ↑
    2

    Move the harvest tray

    Take the lowest mature working tray from above the lower peat buffer and place it on top of the remaining working trays.

  3. ↟ ↟ ↟
    3

    Fluff under light

    Expose the tray to bright ambient light and gently fluff the mature contents so straggling worms can move downward.

  4. ☞
    4

    Skim handfuls

    Remove castings gradually from the top. Pause as needed; light drives worms down through the tray toward the active tray below. Restore the top peat tray and lid afterward.

The lower dry-peat tray never becomes the harvest tray. If castings are still fibrous, wet, sour, or full of food, leave the working tray in service and reassess later. The reviewer’s eventual two-week harvest cadence describes a mature, productive system—not a beginner target.

Conventional alternatives · subordinate

Other workflows described in the reviews

These options are documented for context. They are alternatives to the preferred no-dig, two-buffer method and should not be combined into one sequence.

Alternative A

Conventional upward migration

At roughly two-thirds full, add a prepared working tray above the current one and allow worms to migrate upward. Some keep damp paper over the top working tray as a fly barrier instead of dedicating a dry-peat tray.

Alternative B

Reviewer’s downward contact column

Place a new coir-mat working tray below, build a one-sided column of moved castings and worms so trays touch, align old material above it, then feed only the lower tray. This separate method used spacers to maintain contact and level support.

The whole method

Dry above.
Damp within.
Dry below.

Feed one covered surface pile weekly, keep the three working trays airy, treat drainage as a fault, and use light—not hand-sorting—to harvest the lowest mature working tray.
